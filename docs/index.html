Play a world model trained in 10 minutes for $0.1 of compute

Every frame below is generated by a neural network on your GPU, one keypress at a time. tinywmcraft is a 14M-parameter world model of Craftax, a 2D Minecraft, learned from pixels and keys alone. It is also an open benchmark: train your own world model in under 10 minutes on one A100 and beat our best score!

how it works?

Craftax-Classic is a fast 2D take on Minecraft: walk, chop trees, craft, fight mobs, get a diamond and stay alive! A frozen pretrained RL agent collected 500k frame transitions.

Then tinywmcraft learns a neural network world model of the game. Given a history of frames and an action, it generates the next frame, the reward and whether the episode ends. Feed its own frames back in and you can play inside it!

The model has 13.9M parameters: 5.2M in the tokenizer, 8.6M in the world model. A cold training run plus the eval is about 12 minutes end to end on a single A100, about $0.1 of Colab compute.

what there is to do

Craftax-Classic has 22 achievements. The first unlock of each is worth +1 reward, and the world model has to predict it from pixels: that is the square and the +1 in the player. The score counts them.

Each arrow is a hard requirement in the game code. Tools are unlocks, not ingredients: a pickaxe lets you mine the next material. Iron tools need iron, coal, a furnace and a table nearby. The dim ones on the right can happen any time.

the speedrun

Beat the record gFVD in one 600-second A100 run.

data
train.npz on Hugging Face, 500k transitions. Nothing else.
compute
600 s on one A100 40GB, everything included: tokenizer, data loading, compilation. No compile cache.
eval
eval.py is frozen. Loading your model, compiling and generating must take at most 180 s on the maintainer's Colab A100. Any model and any sampler, as long as it generates one frame per action.
seeds
The score is the mean over training seeds 0, 1 and 2.
gates
gFVD beats the record by a noise-aware margin. AF, reward correlation, reward AP and done AUC stay above fixed floors, 95% of the train_tiny.py baseline. Records are ranked on gFVD alone.

To submit:

  1. Train seeds 0, 1 and 2 and score each with eval.py.
  2. Open a pull request with your code, the three ledger rows and links to the three checkpoints on Hugging Face.
  3. The maintainer reruns it next to the record. If it passes the gates, it is the new record.

The full rules are in program.md.

800 experiments

The search took about 800 scored runs, almost all in eight days of September, roughly 115 A100-hours. The ideas fill 172 experiment branches; 131 of them were opened on a single day. Only 14 changes were ever accepted. For scale: copying the last frame scores 133, the first recipe scored 17.7, the record scores 14.7. The biggest enemy turned out to be the compiler. Same code and same seed, compiled fresh, give a different program, because XLA's autotuner picks different GPU kernels each time: seed 0 of one unchanged recipe landed anywhere from 12.6 to 15.0, a bigger swing than almost any idea we tried. One of the best wins needed no training at all: stop adding noise to the real context frames, and the score fell from 18.0 to 16.6. Doubling the sampling steps did nothing.

inside the model

Tokenizer. A 63×63 frame becomes a 4×4×32 latent. The 81 tiles of 7×7 pixels are tokens for six transformer blocks, then a stride-2 conv and four residual conv blocks. The decoder mirrors it. Training mixes clean, half-masked and noised frames and always reconstructs the clean one.

World model. Six transformer blocks of width 256 alternate spatial attention over the 16 latent tokens with causal attention across 32 frames. The action and each frame's noise level condition adaLN-zero blocks. Flow matching predicts the clean latent, and eight Euler steps generate each frame.

Heads. A reward head (two-hot over 255 symlog bins) and a termination head read each frame's mean token, so the model returns frames, rewards and episode ends.

Optimizer. Muon on every matrix and conv kernel, AdamW on embeddings, biases and norms. Warmup, constant, then linear decay over the last 20% of steps.

Kernels. At width 256 a training step is bound by memory traffic, so attention, modulated norms, gated residuals and GELU run as fused Pallas kernels on NVIDIA GPUs, with an XLA fallback elsewhere.

evaluation

The frozen evaluator takes 2048 windows from held-out episodes, primes the model on eight real frames and generates 16 frames open-loop.

gFVD ↓ (×10⁴) is the Fréchet distance between real and generated 4-frame clips in the feature space of a fixed random CNN. It needs no external checkpoint and is not comparable to I3D FVD.

AF ↑ is action following: the share of windows where the true actions predict the salient pixels better than random actions, under the same context and noise. A model that ignores actions scores 0.5.

Heads are scored by reward correlation, reward AP, reward event recall and precision, and done AUC, both on real frames and on the model's own imagined rollout.

the whole model on one page

train_tiny.py is the complete model in one file: tokenizer, world model, heads and training. Under 250 lines, no custom kernels. Read it on screen or print it as a poster.
